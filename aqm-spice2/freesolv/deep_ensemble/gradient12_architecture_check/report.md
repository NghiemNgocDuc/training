## Check 1: graph diameter / over-squashing (H9)

RDKit heavy-atom bond graph from SMILES: diameter, mean shortest-path length
(unordered pairs), n atoms with shortest path to the graph center > 4 hops
(the model has 4 interaction blocks).

| metric | gradient12 med | certain47 med | MWU p |
|---|---|---|---|
| diameter | 4 | 5 | 0.30 |
| mean shortest-path length | 2.07 | 2.33 | 0.27 |
| n atoms beyond 4 hops | 0 | 0 | 0.64 |

- No group separation. Beyond-4-hop atoms: zero in both groups — every atom is
  within 4 hops of the graph center, so over-squashing cannot even arise for the
  model's 4 interaction blocks on this test set.
- Spearman vs |error| across all 129: diameter rho = −0.225 (p = 0.087),
  mean_spl rho = −0.244 (p = 0.063) — marginal, and in the OPPOSITE direction
  of the mechanism (smaller/shallower graphs trend toward LARGER errors).

**Verdict: no evidence for over-squashing; hypothesis 9 ruled out.**

cancellation ratio = max(|E_gas|, |E_solv|)/|dG| (option-B output IS dG_solv,

- Spearman: |E_solv| vs |error| rho = −0.267 (p = 0.041); cancellation ratio

**Verdict: no group difference; hypothesis 10 ruled out. (Weak continuous
trend: better cancellation ↔ smaller error, uncorrected.)**

## Check 3: interaction-cutoff sensitivity (H11)

Stored-hdf5 conformers, cutoff 6.0 Å, 1.5× proximity = 9.0 Å; RDKit SMILES+AddHs
bond graph index-aligned with the hdf5 (element order verified):
- `n_through_space` = pairs 3D-close (< 9.0 Å) but > 4 bonds apart in graph
- `n_invisible_bonded` = pairs ≤ 4 bonds apart but 3D distance > 6.0 Å (i.e.
  covalent interaction paths truncated by the cutoff)

| metric | gradient12 med | certain47 med | MWU p |
|---|---|---|---|
| n through-space pairs | 16.5 | 45 | 0.71 |
| n invisible-bonded pairs | 0 | 0 | (degenerate) |

- `n_invisible_bonded` is ZERO for every molecule: the 6.0 Å cutoff never
  truncates a bonded interaction path in these stored conformers — the cutoff is
  generous for these small molecules. Structural null, not a measurement failure.
- `n_through_space`: no group separation (p = 0.71; direction OPPOSITE — g12 has
  FEWER through-space contacts). vs |error| n.s. (rho = −0.192, p = 0.15).
- Interesting continuous signal vs SIGNED error: rho = +0.389 (p = 0.002,
  uncorrected) — molecules with more through-space contacts are over-predicted
  (positive signed error). This is the strongest correlation in the whole
  analysis, but it (a) does not separate g12 from c47, (b) predicts directional
  bias, not error magnitude. Not a gradient-12 explanation.

**Verdict: no group difference; hypothesis 11 ruled out. (Footnote: through-
space contact count correlates with over-prediction across all molecules,
rho = 0.39, p = 0.002 uncorrected — not g12-specific.)**

## Bottom line

Three architecture-level mechanisms ruled out: graph over-squashing (H9),
energy-cancellation magnitude (H10), interaction-cutoff truncation (H11).

Total rule-outs across the investigation: 11 — Tanimoto similarity, GMM-NLL
confidence, physicochemical descriptors, sign consistency, conformer-ensemble
instability, data-source provenance, conformer-draw sensitivity, conformer-seed
ensembling, over-squashing, energy cancellation, cutoff truncation.

The gradient-12 cluster has survived every controlled comparison we can run
against the surviving artifacts. The one recurring continuous correlation —
through-space contact count vs over-prediction (rho ≈ 0.39) — is worth a look in
a future dataset/model, but it does not distinguish the gradient-12 molecules.
